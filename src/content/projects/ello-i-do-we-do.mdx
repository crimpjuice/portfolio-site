*Ello · AI Learning Designer, Math (Contractor) · K–2 math · 2026*

## The problem

Two connected activities, *Puddlewump Pond Fish Count* (the "I do" demonstration) and *Captain Barnacle's Submarine* (the "We do" guided practice), were meant to teach one concept. They explained it differently, which confused kids, and neither was as engaging as it should have been. For many children this was also their first introduction to a new measurement tool.

## My role

I owned the revision of both activities: the teaching flow, how the AI tutor was instructed to behave in each one, the interactive widgets, and the engagement. I worked alongside the math learning designer who led the activity.

## What I did

- Aligned both activities so the "I do" modelled the concept and the "We do" practised it, using the same language and the same steps.
- Added a short first explanation in the "I do" and a quick review in the "We do", because many kids were seeing the concept for the first time.
- Kept the story and characters kids already liked, and reworked the mechanics underneath them.
- Tested each flow end-to-end and recorded Loom walkthroughs for subject-matter-expert review.

## What I learned from review

**Emotional safety beats theme fit.** Fishing hooks and poles fit the pond story, but they could scare young kids. Those assets were redesigned.

**Images have to match the learning goal.** Some visuals pulled kids toward measurement when the target skill was graphing. In early learning, a picture tells a child what the task is about.

## Evidence of impact

> Teaching activities for young children only work when the story, the visuals, and the teaching all point at one skill. I now check each asset against the learning objective before it goes to production, and I get expert eyes on an activity before assets are commissioned.

## Transferable skills demonstrated

I do → We do → You do sequencing, early-childhood math pedagogy, voice-first interaction design, prompting an AI tutor's behaviour per activity, and multimedia learning principles applied to a real product with real constraints.
