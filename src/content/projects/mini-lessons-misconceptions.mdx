## The problem

Some errors come back every semester. Students hold a consistent but wrong model of how the world works, and telling them the right answer doesn't change it. The wrong model has to come out and be tested first.

## How each lesson works

1. **Draw out the misconception.** A scenario gets students to commit to the common wrong prediction.
2. **Let it fail.** The lesson shows what actually happens and makes the gap hard to explain away.
3. **Give the correct model.** A clear explanation that points at the exact feature the wrong model missed.
4. **Check transfer.** A new context with the same underlying idea.

Each lesson targets one documented misconception, chosen from past exam item analysis, physics education research and errors I saw in class. The lessons run inside D2L as SCORM packages.

## What changed

Students who completed these lessons did measurably better on related exam items than earlier cohorts. In their reflections, they could explain *why* their first answer was wrong, which is stronger evidence of change than a correct answer alone.
