## The design problem

Practice is the mechanism through which physics skill is built — there's no shortcut. But traditional problem sets create an aversive relationship with practice: the feedback is delayed (you find out you were wrong days later), the experience of being stuck has no support, and there's no sense of progress or accomplishment. Students avoid them.

Gamified problem sets are designed to make the same practice intrinsically motivating.

## Design decisions

### Immediate feedback as the core mechanic

Every answer gets immediate feedback — not just right or wrong, but a brief explanation of *why*. This transforms practice from a performance ("am I right?") into a learning loop ("what did I just learn?"). Immediate feedback is one of the most robustly supported findings in learning science.

### Visible progress within the session

Students can see how far through the problem set they are and how many they've answered correctly. This addresses the "infinite treadmill" feeling of traditional homework — there's a clear beginning, middle, and end, and a sense of momentum as they move through it.

### Challenge structure with increasing difficulty

Problems are sequenced from accessible to challenging within each set. Students experience early success before encountering harder items — a deliberate design choice based on self-efficacy research. Struggling on problem 8 feels different when you've already succeeded on problems 1 through 5.

### Targeted to specific topics and student interests

Problem sets were generated with AI assistance to align with specific course topics and, where possible, with student-declared interests — making the scenarios feel relevant rather than arbitrary.

## Evidence of impact

> Students reported increased willingness to attempt problems independently. Voluntary engagement with optional practice sets was higher than with equivalent traditional problem sets in prior semesters.
