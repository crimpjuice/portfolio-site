Most labs tell students what to measure and what to conclude. These labs give students a goal and let them work out the rest.

## Discovery labs (PHYS 104 and PHYS 295)

Each lab has one objective, no procedure and no equations. The struggle that follows is where the learning happens.

**Foucault's pendulum.** Students watched a video of the pendulum in the Panthéon in Paris and got a small pendulum of their own. The task: *determine the length and mass of the Foucault pendulum.* They tested mass, length and angle, and found that mass didn't change the period. The question "why doesn't mass matter?" came from their own data. They then predicted the Panthéon pendulum's properties and checked them against the video.

**Room temperature from sound.** Students got a standing-wave apparatus and one task: find the temperature of the room. That means linking resonance to the speed of sound, and the speed of sound to temperature. Bonus marks went to proper uncertainty and to checking the result a second way.

**Laser frequency from diffraction (PHYS 295).** Students got a He-Ne laser, slits and gratings, and had to find the laser's frequency with uncertainty. They chose which slits to use, with one rule: *make choices that decrease your error.* Reports had to name the specific cause of any discrepancy. "Human error" did not count.

## The customized lab: the arm as a lever (PHYS 104)

Many students in this section were headed into medicine, nursing and kinesiology. A metre stick with hanging masses teaches torque, but it means little to them. So they modelled their own forearm as a lever. Students:

- measured their forearm and found where the bicep tendon attaches, about 4–6 cm from the elbow
- predicted the bicep force needed to hold a load at 90 degrees
- measured that force with a spring scale across six loads and compared the two

**The surprise:** the bicep pulls with many times the force of the load. The arm trades force for speed and range of motion. Discussion questions compared levers across the body and across animals, from a calf raise to a cheetah's leg.
