I teach people how to think through hard problems. Physics is the medium. These six principles guide what I build, in a classroom or in a product.

## Six principles

**1. Design for cognitive load.** Working memory is small (Sweller). Before I build anything, I ask what the learner has to hold in mind at once, and I cut whatever doesn't serve the goal.

**2. Measure, then iterate.** Every design gets a feedback loop: engagement data at Brilliant.org, support requests and LMS data at Camosun, drop-off data at Ello.

**3. Build guardrails into AI tools.** A tutor that gives answers teaches dependence. A tutor that asks the right question at the right moment teaches reasoning.

**4. Hold tools loosely.** Platforms change every few months. Three questions last longer: what problem am I solving, what does this tool make possible, and what judgement does it still need from me?

**5. Build confidence early.** Students who believe they can't do math stop trying. My tools start with early wins, then raise the difficulty.

**6. Design for what people do best together.** Curiosity, collaboration and the pride of getting good at something hard drive deep learning. AI handles the logistics, so class time can go to these.

## The research behind principle 6

- **Curiosity** improves memory, even for unrelated material learned in the same state (Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath, 2014).
- **Relevance and intrinsic motivation** are among the strongest predictors of persistence (Self-Determination Theory; Howard et al., 2021, over 200,000 students).
- **Interest can be grown.** Interest sparked by a good classroom moment can become lasting (Hidi & Renninger).
- **Peers matter.** Working through problems together produces richer learning than working alone (Vygotsky).
- **Well-designed difficulty** improves long-term retention and transfer (Bjork & Bjork, 1994; Kapur, 2008).

This research is decades old. What AI changed is the cost. Rewriting problems around each student's interests, or answering a misconception the same day, used to take more time than one instructor had.

## The classroom redesign (PHYS 104)

AI sits in the top layer. It frees time for the bottom layer. Here is what changed in the course:

- **Flipped structure.** Students meet the content before class, and class time goes to active work.
- **Underspecified problems.** Students decide what they need to know before they solve.
- **Live demos** tied to problems. Students predict, observe, then reconcile.
- **Peer instruction** and structured group work.
- **Problems matched to interests,** generated with AI from a day-one survey.
- **AI-assisted grouping** around shared interests.
- **Same-day mini-lessons** when a misconception shows up in class.
- **A Productive Struggle grade,** described below.

## What I noticed

One semester is not a controlled study. I tracked surveys, attendance, office-hour questions, unit test scores and weekly reflections, and the patterns were consistent:

- Students asked better questions, about edge cases and connections between ideas.
- Their mistakes became more sophisticated.
- Attendance went up.
- Test scores improved from unit to unit.
- Office-hour questions shifted from "how do I do this?" to "why does this work?"

## The Productive Struggle grade

15% of the grade rewards honest effort on hard problems. Each week, students answer six questions on D2L in under two minutes. The questions cover three needs from Self-Determination Theory: **autonomy** (choosing how to attack a problem), **competence** (persisting and revising) and **relatedness** (working with others). I calibrate the scores lightly from what I see in class. Later weeks count more, and the lowest week is dropped.

> You do not need to always be correct. You need to always be trying.

**Weekly self-assessment (D2L, due Friday)**

| # | Question | Need | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | This week I attempted problems even when I didn't know where to start. | Autonomy | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 2 | When I got stuck, I kept working rather than waiting for the answer. | Competence | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 3 | I contributed to my group's thinking, even if I wasn't sure I was right. | Relatedness | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 4 | I revised my understanding when I got feedback or saw a different approach. | Competence | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 5 | I helped someone else in this class get unstuck, or let someone help me. | Relatedness | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 6 | What was one moment this week where you pushed through something hard? | All | 2–3 sentences |

**Weekly score**

| Score | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| 4, Thriving | Attempted hard problems, stayed engaged when stuck, contributed to the group all week. |
| 3, Growing | Mostly attempted problems and stayed engaged, with occasional lapses. |
| 2, Developing | Some attempts at hard work; relied on others more than trying first. |
| 1, Beginning | Rarely attempted challenging problems; often disengaged. |
| 0, Absent | Not present, or not participating. |
