## What this grade is for

Physics is learned by doing — especially by doing hard things imperfectly. This component recognizes something that tests and labs cannot easily capture: your willingness to engage with difficulty, revise your thinking, and keep working when you don't yet have the answer.

> You do not need to always be correct. You need to always be trying.

This grade rewards:

- Attempting problems even when you're unsure where to start
- Staying engaged when you get stuck instead of waiting for the answer
- Contributing to group thinking, even with an uncertain idea
- Revising your understanding when you receive feedback
- Supporting your peers' thinking and learning

This grade does not penalize:

- Being wrong
- Being quiet — engagement takes many forms
- Struggling — that's the point

## The three dimensions

Productive struggle isn't one thing. Decades of motivation research — Ryan and Deci's Self-Determination Theory, supported by a meta-analysis of over 200,000 students (Howard et al., 2021) — find that people sustain difficult work when three conditions are met. This instrument is built on those three, and every question below maps to one of them.

| Dimension | What it means here | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Autonomy** | You made real choices about how to attack the problem, rather than waiting to be shown a single correct path. | Trying a method before you know it's right; deciding what information you need; choosing where to start on an underspecified problem. |
| **Competence** | You built genuine capability over time on work that was appropriately hard — not just produced correct answers. | Staying with a problem when stuck; revising a wrong model after feedback; being able to say *why* you changed your answer. |
| **Relatedness** | You worked *with* people rather than merely beside them. | Contributing an uncertain idea to your group; asking a peer a clarifying question; explaining your reasoning to someone stuck. |

Relatedness carries real weight here, and that's deliberate. Research on belonging in STEM finds that students point to collaborative struggle — not content delivery — as what made them feel they belonged in their major, and that fast-paced, solo-only courses produce isolation instead. Working through hard physics alongside other people isn't a soft add-on to this course. It's one third of what this grade measures.

## How it works

Each week you complete a short self-assessment on D2L — six questions, under two minutes. Your score for that week is based primarily on your own reflection, calibrated by instructor observation during class.

Instructor calibration is light: I circulate during class activities and note patterns across the group. Self-scores that seem significantly misaligned with observed behaviour may be adjusted — up or down. Students who consistently self-assess honestly will rarely be overridden.

> Think of this as a weekly check-in with yourself, not a performance for the instructor.

## Weekly scoring rubric

Each week is scored out of 4 based on your self-assessment and instructor calibration:

| Weekly score | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| 4 — Thriving | Consistently attempted hard problems; stayed engaged when stuck; contributed to group thinking; demonstrated persistence all week. |
| 3 — Growing | Mostly attempted problems; engaged with group; showed persistence most of the time; occasional disengagement. |
| 2 — Developing | Some attempts at difficult work; participation was inconsistent; relied on peers/instructor more than trying first. |
| 1 — Beginning | Rarely attempted challenging problems; disengaged for significant portions of class; hard to see productive struggle occurring. |
| 0 — Absent | Not present, or present but not participating in any meaningful way. |

## Scores are weighted toward growth

Because this course rewards growth over perfection, later weeks carry more weight. A student who starts slow and builds real engagement will do better than one who coasts from the start.

| Weeks | Weight | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | 1x | Getting your feet under you |
| Weeks 3–5 | 1.5x | Building momentum |
| Weeks 6–7 | 2x | Demonstrating growth |

The lowest single week's score will also be dropped to account for illness, emergencies, or an off week.

## Weekly D2L self-assessment

Submitted on D2L by end of day Friday each week. Takes under 2 minutes. Completion is required to receive a score for that week.

| # | Question | Dimension | Response type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | This week I attempted problems even when I didn't know where to start. | Autonomy | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 2 | When I got stuck, I kept working rather than waiting for the answer. | Competence | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 3 | I contributed to my group's thinking, even if I wasn't sure I was right. | Relatedness | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 4 | I revised my understanding when I got feedback or saw a different approach. | Competence | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 5 | I helped someone else in this class get unstuck, or let someone help me. | Relatedness | Never / Sometimes / Consistently |
| 6 (open) | What was one moment this week where you pushed through something hard? (2–3 sentences) | — | Short text |

> Question 6 is the most important. A specific, honest answer — even about a moment where you struggled and didn't succeed — is exactly what this question is looking for.

## What engagement looks like in this class

Because this is a flipped, in-person course, engagement happens in many different ways. All of the following count:

**During problem-solving activities**

- Writing something down before asking for help
- Trying a method even if you're not sure it's right
- Asking "what if we tried…" with your group
- Identifying where you got stuck and articulating why

**During group work**

- Contributing an idea, even tentatively
- Asking a clarifying question of a peer
- Quietly working through your own reasoning while the group discusses
- Explaining your thinking to someone who's stuck

**During whiteboard or class-wide activities**

- Attempting the problem before the solution is shown
- Changing your answer and being able to say why
- Asking a question about something that confused you

## Logistics & policies

**Submission deadline.** Weekly D2L self-assessments are due Friday by 11:59 PM. Late submissions receive a score of 0 for that week.

**Missed weeks.** The lowest week is dropped automatically. Beyond that, a score of 0 applies for missed submissions. In cases of serious illness or emergency, contact me before the deadline where possible.

**Grade disputes.** If your instructor-calibrated score differs from your self-assessment and you disagree, bring it up within one week. I'm happy to discuss what I observed and hear your perspective.

> The student who earns full marks here is not the loudest in the room or the fastest with answers. It's the one who shows up ready, tries hard things, reflects honestly, and keeps going when it's difficult. That's the physicist you're becoming.
